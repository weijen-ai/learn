Percent change

This page is a template. Its content is deliberately simple and original so the interactive pattern is easy to see: objectives, an explanation that builds up in steps, a formula, flashcards, and a short quiz.

Learning objectives

After this unit you should be able to:

  • explain what a percent change measures and why the old value is the reference;
  • compute a percent change from two numbers, including a decrease;
  • recognise the common mistake of dividing by the new value.

Explanation, step by step

Open each step in order. Each one adds a little to the last.

Step 1 · What question does a percent change answer?

A price goes from 40 to 50. Saying “it went up by 10” is true, but 10 means something different for a 40-dollar item than for a 4,000-dollar item. A percent change compares the difference to where you started.

So the question is: the change is what fraction of the old value?

Step 2 · The formula

Write the old value as $\text{old}$ and the new value as $\text{new}$. Then

$$\text{percent change} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{old}}{\text{old}} \times 100\%$$

The numerator is the change. The denominator is the reference: always the old value.

Step 3 · A worked example (and a decrease)

From 40 to 50: $\frac{50 - 40}{40} \times 100\% = \frac{10}{40} \times 100\% = 25\%$.

From 50 back to 40: $\frac{40 - 50}{50} \times 100\% = -20\%$. The same 10 is now a smaller share, because the reference is 50, and the sign tells you it is a decrease.

Check yourself: going up 25% and then down 20% returns you to the start. Percent changes do not simply cancel.

Flashcards

Read the prompt, answer in your head, then reveal. With JavaScript on, the cards are shown one at a time.

Which value goes in the denominator of a percent change?

Show answer
The old value. The change is measured relative to where you started.

A quantity goes from 80 to 100. What is the percent change?

Show answer
$\frac{100-80}{80}\times100\% = 25\%$.

What does a negative percent change mean?

Show answer
A decrease: the new value is smaller than the old one.

Quick quiz

Pick an answer to get immediate feedback.

1. A price rises from 60 to 75. What is the percent change?
2. A value falls from 200 to 150. What is the percent change?
3. Someone computes the change from 40 to 50 as 20%. What went wrong?
